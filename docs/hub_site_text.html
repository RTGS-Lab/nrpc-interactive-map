  1. Select an area: Click Zoom to... and choose one or more cities, or select Full County to use the entire Hennepin County boundary. Click Zoom to fit the map to your selection.
  2. Turn on datasets: Open the Layers panel and toggle on a layer group — all layers within it will turn on automatically and the group will expand. Use the search bar to find a specific layer.
  3. Adjust transparency: Click the sliders icon next to any layer or group to open its opacity slider. Use the Basemap dropdown to switch background maps or adjust basemap opacity.
  4. Export data: Click Clip & Zip — the panel will prompt you if a selection or visible layer is still needed. Once ready, choose SHP or GeoJSON, optionally name the file, and click Export to download a ZIP of all visible non-Boundaries datasets clipped to your selected area.

Use Case #1 — Internal Use Only (Single Organization Planning → Action)

Objective: Enable a single jurisdiction (e.g., county, city, watershed district) to translate existing prioritization models into implementation-ready projects.

Primary Users:

  • Planning staff
  • Natural resources staff
  • GIS analysts
  • Program managers

Inputs:

  • Metropolitan Council prioritization layers
  • Hennepin County prioritization layers (e.g., conservation easements)
  • Internal datasets (parcels, infrastructure, known problem areas)

Workflow — Steps 1–2

Step 1 — Define Scope:

  • Select jurisdiction boundary (auto-filter layers)
  • Optionally segment by subwatershed, land use type, or program area (e.g., habitat, water quality)

Step 2 — Activate Prioritization Layers:

  • Toggle Met Council model outputs and County prioritization layers
  • Adjust opacity / stacking to identify high-value overlaps

Workflow — Steps 3–4

Step 3 — Identify Candidate Opportunities:

  • Staff visually scan + query high-priority clusters and gaps in current programming
  • Use filtering tools: parcel ownership, land cover, impairment status

Step 4 — Create "Opportunity Points":

  • Add point or polygon to the Opportunities Layer
  • Required attributes: title, description, priority level, program alignment, estimated feasibility, staff owner

Workflow — Steps 5–6 & Outputs

Step 5 — Internal Review + Refinement: Weekly or sprint-based review to validate feasibility, remove duplicates, and rank opportunities.

Step 6 — Plan Development: Export map layers and opportunity dataset to feed into Comprehensive Plans, capital improvement planning, and grant targeting.


Outputs: Structured opportunity inventory, spatially explicit project list, plan-aligned implementation pipeline.

Use Case #2 — Internal + 1–2 Close Partners (Coordinated Planning)

Objective: Coordinate across a small set of aligned entities to identify shared opportunities and reduce fragmentation.

Primary Users:

  • County + watershed district
  • Adjacent municipalities
  • Implementation partners

Additional Inputs:

  • Partner datasets (e.g., watershed plans, capital plans)
  • Shared priorities (e.g., TMDLs, flood mitigation zones)

Workflow — Steps 1–2

Step 1 — Joint Scope Definition:

  • Select combined multi-jurisdiction geography
  • Define shared goals (e.g., phosphorus reduction, habitat corridors)

Step 2 — Shared Layer Environment:

  • Enable all Level 1 layers, partner-specific layers, and a "jurisdiction ownership" overlay

Workflow — Steps 3–4

Step 3 — Joint Opportunity Identification:

  • Partners independently add Opportunity Points
  • Tag each with organization and collaboration potential (low / medium / high)

Step 4 — Structured Coordination:

  • Use facilitated sessions to review overlaps
  • Identify redundant efforts and synergies
  • Merge or split opportunities as needed

Workflow — Steps 5–6

Step 5 — Assign Roles:

  • For each opportunity, define lead organization, supporting partners, and coordination needs

Step 6 — Prioritize Cross-Boundary Projects:

  • Rank based on multi-benefit impact, funding leverage, and implementation feasibility

Workflow — Step 7 & Outputs

Step 7 — Export + Alignment: Outputs feed into multiple plans simultaneously, joint funding proposals, and interagency agreements.


Outputs: Coordinated opportunity map, reduced duplication, defined ownership + collaboration structure.

Use Case #3 — Full Participatory Mapping (Planning + Public Engagement + Co-Production)

Objective: Integrate community, stakeholders, and practitioners into the identification of opportunities—while maintaining technical rigor.

Primary Users:

  • All Level 2 users
  • Community members
  • NGOs
  • Landowners
  • Contractors

Additional Inputs:

  • Workshop outputs
  • Community feedback
  • Field observations
  • Local knowledge

Workflow — Steps 1–2

Step 1 — Prepare Public-Facing Map:

  • Simplify layer stack to a curated subset of technical layers
  • Enable pop-ups with plain-language explanations and guided navigation

Step 2 — Conduct Workshops:

  • In-person or virtual sessions walking through priority areas and existing constraints
  • Participants identify opportunities and flag concerns

Workflow — Steps 3–4

Step 3 — Capture Participatory Input:

  • Add features via facilitator-led or direct user input
  • Required attributes: opportunity type, local context, contact (optional)

Step 4 — Validate + Filter:

  • Internal review to remove infeasible suggestions and flag high-value local insights

Workflow — Steps 5–6

Step 5 — Integrate with Technical Layers:

  • Overlay participatory inputs with prioritization models
  • Identify high alignment (priority + community support) and conflicts

Step 6 — Final Opportunity Set:

  • Combine technical opportunities and participatory inputs
  • Tag each as community-supported or high-priority technical

Workflow — Step 7 & Outputs

Step 7 — Feedback Loop: Share results back via public dashboards and reports to maintain transparency.


Outputs: Socially validated project list, increased legitimacy and buy-in, improved implementation success.

Navigate

  • Click and drag to pan; scroll or use + / - (top right) to zoom.
  • Click the Home icon to return to the default map view.
  • Click the Fullscreen icon (top right, above zoom) to expand the map to fill the browser window.

Legend & Layers

  • Click Legend or Layers to toggle those panels open or closed. The Legend opens automatically when you turn on your first dataset.
  • In the Layers panel, toggle a layer group on to make it and all layers within it visible. Use the search bar to find a layer by name.
  • Click the sliders icon next to any layer or group to open its opacity slider.

Basemap

  • Click Basemap to switch between Light Gray Canvas, Imagery, and Navigation. Use the opacity slider in the dropdown to adjust the basemap transparency.

Zoom to...

  • Click Zoom to... to open a searchable list of cities — or select Full County to use the entire Hennepin County boundary. Selected areas are highlighted in blue.
  • Click Zoom to fit the map to your selection, or Clear to reset.

Clip & Zip

  • Click Clip & Zip at any time. The panel will indicate if a city/county selection or visible dataset is still needed before exporting.
  • Choose SHP or GeoJSON, optionally enter a file name, then click Export. All visible non-Boundaries layers intersecting your selected area will download as a ZIP file.
